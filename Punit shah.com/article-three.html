What is your morning ritual?

How a team starts its day can set the tone for everything that follows. I had been trying to establish an effective morning ritual at work for a long time. My initial thoughts drifted toward conventional methods: a summary email sent each evening, a follow-up meeting to review yesterday’s activities, or even a weekly "champion" meeting. While these ideas had merit, they lacked the energy and connection I wanted to foster.

2 min read
Punit Shah

After much thought and some inspiration from watching videos on how other organizations operate, we decided to try morning huddles. The concept was simple, but its execution brought up important questions. A ritual is easy to start, but sustaining it is the real challenge. Would the content be interesting to everyone? How would we handle confidential information? I knew that for this to work, it had to be more than just another meeting. It needed to become a source of energy and inspiration for the entire team.

The Evolution of the Morning Huddle

We dove into our new routine of huddling every morning. Initially, it felt a bit structured and repetitive. To break the monotony and make the sessions more meaningful, we introduced a new requirement: each team member had to share their “aha” moment from the previous day. This was a turning point. We encouraged everyone to share wins from both their work and personal lives.

The results were transformative. The team began to genuinely look forward to hearing about the small miracles and breakthroughs their colleagues experienced. A positive side effect quickly became apparent: as team members shared personal stories, their communication skills naturally improved. There’s something powerful about personal narratives that brings out the communicator in all of us. The huddles became less about reporting and more about connecting.

Building a Cohesive and Inclusive Team

These daily huddles did more than just improve communication; they made our team more cohesive and tightly-knit. The practice created a safe space where everyone’s voice was heard and valued. New team members found it easy to integrate into this egalitarian culture, as the morning ritual immediately made them feel like part of the family.

We kept things fresh by adding fun twists. Some days, we would have team members exchange their "aha" moments and read them aloud, letting others guess whose update it was. This simple game added a layer of fun and encouraged active listening. The huddles became a cornerstone of our company culture, fostering a sense of community that is often hard to build in a busy work environment.

The Ripple Effect of Positivity

The practice of sharing “aha” moments had a profound ripple effect. Team members started actively looking for and noting their positive experiences. Some were inspired to work towards creating their own miracles, while others found creative ways to "manufacture" them. This daily dose of positivity fueled our collective energy and creativity. Miracles, big and small, started making our days at City One.

This internal culture of positivity and celebration aligns perfectly with our external philosophy. At City One, we believe in giving more to our clients. Instead of asking, "What's in it for us?" we focus on, "What's in it for them?" This client-centric approach is the DNA of our organization, helping us build a base of delighted customers who become our biggest advocates. The morning huddles reinforce this mindset by reminding us daily of the power of positive impact and shared success.
